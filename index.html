🆘 Found an Animal? Do This First

Your safety first, then the animal's. Most wild animals need a trained rescuer more than they need your help.

First 6 steps for ANY wild animal

  1. Keep a safe distance. Do not touch large or unknown animals (leopard, monkey, snake, deer, bear).
  2. Call the forest department / rescue helpline right away (choose your state below).
  3. Reduce noise and crowds. Less light and less sound means less stress. Keep children and pets away.
  4. Pick up only if necessary (on a road, in a drain, in water). Cover small animals with a towel or cloth and protect your hands with cloth.
  5. Place it in a dark, quiet, ventilated box (cardboard box with air holes and a soft cloth at the bottom).
  6. Do not force food or water. The wrong food or forced water can kill. Follow the rescuer's advice.

✅ Do

  • Tell the helpline the location, type of animal and its condition
  • Take photos or video from a distance (no flash)
  • Protect the animal from heat and cold
  • Keep pets and children away
  • Stay nearby or keep updating the rescuer until help arrives

❌ Don't

  • Do not keep a wild animal as a pet (illegal under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972)
  • Do not force milk, bread, roti or human food
  • Do not keep picking it up or petting it
  • Do not crowd around it for selfies
  • Do not apply medicine or ointment yourself

Animal-wise first aid (until the rescuer arrives)

This is general guidance. Every case is different, so the rescuer's or vet's advice always comes first.

🐦 Baby bird (nestling / fledgling)

First, identify it

  • Fledgling: has feathers and hops around. Its parents are usually nearby. Mostly leave it alone, or move it only onto a nearby bush or branch if it is in danger.
  • Nestling: bare or fuzzy with very few feathers. If you can find the nest, put it back. Parent birds do not abandon chicks because of human touch.

If it is injured or alone

  • Line a small cardboard box with a soft cloth or tissue and make air holes in the lid.
  • Keep the box in a quiet, dark, room-temperature place. To keep it warm, wrap a bottle of lukewarm (not hot) water in cloth and put it in one corner so the bird can move away from it.
  • Do not put water in its beak (it can enter the windpipe). Never give milk.
  • Call the helpline. Do not try to raise it yourself for long.
🦅 Injured adult bird (kite, owl, parrot, pigeon, peacock)
  • Gently cover it with a towel or cloth and pick it up. Watch out for the beak and talons; large birds (kite, owl) can injure you badly.
  • If it is tangled in manjha (kite string) and the wound is deep, do not cut it off yourself. Wait for a rescuer.
  • Keep it in a dark, quiet box. Do not try to straighten or splint a wing or leg.
  • In heat keep it in a cool shaded place.
🐿️ Squirrel / small mammal baby
  • First check whether the mother is nearby. Watch from a distance for a few hours; she may come back.
  • If it is cold, wrap it in a soft cloth and give gentle warmth (a bottle of lukewarm water wrapped in cloth).
  • Do not give cow's milk. It can cause diarrhoea and death. Each species needs a different formula, so ask a rescuer.
  • Do not try to raise it yourself.
🦌 Deer / Nilgai / large animal (road accident, fell in a well)
  • Do not go close. An injured large animal can kick or gore you.
  • Call both the forest department and 112. Share your location pin.
  • Move the crowd back and slow down traffic if it is on a road.
  • If it fell into an open well, put a barrier around the well so nobody else falls in. Only try to pull it out if the rescuer tells you to.
🐍 Snake / reptile (snake, monitor lizard, tortoise)
  • Never catch, hit or tease a snake. Most snakebites happen when people try to catch it.
  • Keep watching from a distance, move people away, and if it is inside a house close the door to keep it in one room.
  • Call a snake rescuer / forest department / Wildlife SOS.
  • Tortoise: if it is on a road, carry it across in the direction it was heading. Do not keep it at home for long.
  • If someone is bitten: go to a hospital or call 112 immediately. Do not waste time on traditional remedies.
🐒 Monkey / langur / bat / any wild mammal
  • Do not touch it. Bites and scratches carry a risk of infections such as rabies.
  • If bitten, wash the wound with clean water and soap for 15 minutes and see a doctor immediately.
  • Watch from a distance, call the helpline, and do not feed it.
🌡️ Heat / dehydration (helpless bird or animal in summer)
  • Keep it in shade, but not in direct fan air.
  • For birds, keep a little water in a small shallow dish nearby (do not pour it into the beak).
  • On your roof or balcony, keep a shaded bowl of clean water and a few grains so nearby birds survive the heat.

Animals We Often Need To Help

Tiger & Big Cats

Keep your distance, stay quiet, and inform the forest department immediately.

Elephant

Do not go near the herd. Give the helpline the location and keep villagers away.

Deer

They panic and injure themselves easily. Keep it calm and do not touch it.

🌍 Why Animals & Birds Matter

  • 🐦 Birds spread seeds and control insects
  • 🦉 Owls and eagles control rats, the farmer's friends
  • 🐝 Bees and butterflies pollinate, and our food depends on them
  • 🐅 Predators keep the ecosystem balanced
  • 🌳 Healthy forests mean clean air, water and a stable climate
